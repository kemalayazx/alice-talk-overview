# A message, in your hands

**Alice Talk — temporary working title.**

This is the exact narration input for an original illustrated film, synthesized with an existing local system voice. The film introduces an experimental prototype. Proposed routing and general agent authorization research are explicitly labeled.

## 1. A small message. A meaningful connection.

A message can be simple. See you at five. But getting it from one person to another raises bigger questions. Who can read it? Who may contact you? What happens when a phone is offline?

## 2. The journey begins inside the phone.

Imagine the message changing into an unreadable pattern before it leaves the phone. It goes into a locked box. The intended recipient's device is designed to open it. This is our picture of end to end encryption.

## 3. Carry the box. Keep the contents sealed.

A courier carries the sealed box using delivery information. In today's prototype, a relay helps messages travel. It is still an infrastructure dependency. The encryption design aims to protect the content, while delivery information may remain visible.

## 4. A message is not a permission slip.

Your phone checks contact approval and blocking separately from the message's words. Calls, accepting files, and optional conversation history have their own choices. With invitation-only mode configured, the relay also checks identity-key possession and membership.

## 5. One handoff at a time.

Now imagine several couriers, each receiving instructions for the next leg. This illustrates a proposed routing idea to investigate, not today's completed service. A courier may still observe its immediate neighbors. We are not promising an invisible trail.

## 6. When the other phone is away.

If the recipient is offline, encrypted text can wait on a temporary shelf. That shelf has limits and lives in memory. A relay restart can lose waiting messages. It is a temporary queue, not a permanent archive.

## 7. Honest progress, even when it is uncertain.

A box accepted by the relay is not a message read by a person. When a live send has an uncertain outcome, the client does not automatically create a fresh offline send. That avoids a blind second attempt. It is not a guarantee against every duplicate.

## 8. The message becomes a message again.

At the recipient's device, the sealed message becomes readable again. Identity material has local vault protection. Keeping conversation history is a separate, optional choice. Trusted keys and safe devices still matter. A locked box cannot repair a compromised phone.

## 9. Progress comes from finding the awkward cases.

Development corrected specific cancellation, cleanup, and identity-change races. One verified developer run passed seven hundred thirty-seven automated tests. An intermittent offline timeout and an invitation-bookkeeping conflict remain unresolved. Tests guide the work; they do not certify security.

## 10. A helpful assistant should know its limits.

Our next research direction brings people, devices, and artificial intelligence assistants into this picture. An assistant might receive one task, with permission checked outside its own words. That general permission layer is proposed research, not a capability already proven here.

## 11. More connection. More human choice.

Alice Talk is a temporary name for an experimental project. The goal is useful communication with clearer human control. Independent review and broader real-device testing are still needed. Help us test the next step.
